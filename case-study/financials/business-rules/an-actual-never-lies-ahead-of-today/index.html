An actual never lies ahead of today

Title

An actual never lies ahead of today

Rule Statement

Confirming refuses any entry dated in the future — a committed row is a statement about money that has already moved, so its date must be today or earlier.

Summary

Future-dated entries are refused at confirm time with a pointer to the edit flow; only what has really landed can become history.

Description

The boundary between plan and fact is guarded at the confirm moment. An expectation dated in the future has not matured yet — confirming it would write a movement into history that has not happened. The refusal is explicit and tells the owner what to do instead: adjust the entry through the edit flow if the expectation's registered date is simply wrong.

The same refusal guards the interactive confirm of a future-dated rule instance: the date may be shifted down to today — an early-landed bill confirmed sooner than scheduled — but never pushed ahead of today.

This is the confirming face of the general boundary that separates forecasts from history (see A future-dated movement goes to the expected register, not the ledger): where that rule routes future movements to the register, this refusal stops a future movement from entering history at all.

    Authoring a rule by hand

    process-story · for client

    The owner sometimes knows an obligation before the books ever see it: a fresh subscription, a rent change, a yearly insurance premium. Authoring a rule turns that knowledge into a standing schedule directly — one complete statement of description, category, amount and rhythm, or a walk whose questions ask only for what is missing, where pressing Enter keeps the offered answer.

    Two quiet mechanics shape what gets stored. A weekly or biweekly rhythm named by weekday moves the start forward to the first matching day, and every occurrence then lands on that weekday for the schedule's life. No bar compares the start with today: a blank start becomes the authoring day, and a readable past start passes just as freely as a future one — the owner may backdate a rule that was truly running before it was written down.

    Before anything stores, the rule meets the same admission bars as an adopted schedule: a non-empty description, a category the owner approved, a non-zero amount, one of four rhythms, sane day and month ranges, readable dates, and an end never before the start. A refusal names its bar, and the store stays untouched. The saved schedule then confirms itself with the cadence in plain words and the next three dates the forecast will anticipate — yet books nothing. The ledger carries no entry for a rule until a real payment or an explicit confirmation touches it.

    Later life runs through the same store's doors, with the same care. Pause silences a schedule while every field and its history stay stored, and resume brings it back as it stood (paused rules keep their place); removal ends the rule permanently, and only after the owner sees the exact rule and types yes (removal waits for a stated yes). One habit to watch: pressing Enter on the anchor question keeps a silent default, pinning a weekly schedule to whatever weekday the authoring day fell on — the printed cadence line is the only place that shows it.

Process story — Entry removal

process-story · for client

A double recording, a card payment logged twice, a plan no longer expected — the bookkeeper opens the delete command and names the row, or lets it show the last ten past rows and picks from that menu. The typed id is resolved in exactly one place, consulted in strict order: the committed books first, then the planned-entry register, then the row groupings, then the projected occurrences of recurring rules. Nothing is removed without a typed affirmative — bare Enter, an unknown word or an interruption all leave every store untouched. An actual transaction's removal is itself a recorded event: it joins the append-only history carrying the row's complete content as it stood, read from the live books before anything changes, and at every later read-back that stored snapshot is checked against the books — a mismatch stops the books from opening at all rather than letting the history claim a removal it did not make, and the balances of every later row are re-carried so no drift remains. Removing a transfer earns an extra warning, because the savings side moved too. The planning stores keep no such history: a plan is simply struck from its register, a rule occurrence is suppressed by adding its date to the rule's exceptions so the rule keeps running for its other periods, and a grouping dissolves without touching its member rows. There is no undo — a wrongly removed entry is re-recorded anew; the journal is the audit trail of what was taken off the books.

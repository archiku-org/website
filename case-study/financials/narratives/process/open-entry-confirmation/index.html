Process story — Confirmation of landed plans

process-story · for client

The rent or insurance that existed only as a plan has now landed at the bank; the bookkeeper confirms it in one step and is asked nothing — the planned figure commits as it was recorded, because adjusting values is the edit door's job, done beforehand. The confirmation books the money through the same doors as any manual booking, and only after the booking succeeds is the plan struck from the register — a refused confirmation (a plan still dated ahead, or carrying no amount) changes nothing, and an already-settled id is answered with a nothing-to-do note, so repeated confirming can never double-book. A due instance of a recurring rule commits from the rule's own values; if the real booking drifted beyond tolerance, the occurrence is noted as an exception date on the rule so the stale forecast line quietly leaves. A group of landed plans walks its members in date order — the group survives, and members that are still future or amountless are skipped with notes rather than blocking the rest. What the household gains: planned money becomes recorded money, the plan registers shrink, and the journal carries proof of which plan each payment settled.

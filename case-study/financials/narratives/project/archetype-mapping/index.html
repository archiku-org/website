    Archetype mapping

    archetype-mapping · for client

    The archetype is a double-entry-adjacent personal ledger with a forecast layer: a single-household bookkeeping suite that keeps an auditable account of committed money, a parking place for planned money, and a rules engine that turns history into forecast — nothing cloud-served, no multi-user world, just one household's two accounts kept provably true. Four core concepts carry the whole shape.

    Actual versus expected is the load-bearing distinction: a movement either happened (dated today or earlier) and lives in the journaled books, or it is planned (dated ahead) and parks in a register that carries no balances. Nearly every rule and boundary in the project reads as a consequence of that one line.

    Content-hash identity is the integrity archetype: a recording's identity is a hash of its own content plus creation time, appended to a journal that can never be edited — so the books replay their own history rather than remember it.

    Running balances is the everyday payoff: every ledger row carries the balance it produced, so the household position at any date is a read, not a recalculation, and a backdated entry threads itself into the chain and pushes every later balance truthful again.

    The approved category list is budgeting policy made executable — the household's own configured vocabulary is checked fresh at every recording on both the actual and the expected side.

    Two further concepts organize the planner: a rule instance is a projected, virtual occurrence — real in the forecast, never in the books — and the confirmation backlog names what has landed but not yet been confirmed, one queue with one drain path.

    How the concepts live in the components, in the component census's own order:

    • The transaction entry interface is where actual-versus-expected is policed at the front door — it validates, routes by date, and closes the loop with the projected-balance confirmation.
    • The confirmation dispatcher is the only bridge between the two worlds: it commits known values as recorded money without a conversation.
    • The interactive entry editor moves entries across that boundary by their corrected date — future edits turn recorded rows into plans, past-dated plans book into the ledger.
    • The recurrence engine is the forecast layer proper: rule store, projection of virtual instances, and the detection that proposes rules — never writing money itself.
    • The interactive deletion dispatcher is the four-way resolver: each store owns its own removal path, and destructive steps demand a typed affirmative.
    • The journaled ledger engine realizes content-hash identity and running balances — append, replay, verify, carry balances forward.
    • The expected register store is the planned-money world: strictly future-dated, balance-free, struck the moment a plan settles.
    • The cleanup fix engine treats corrections as reviewed, offline-authored change-set files (the change-set concept), each fix guarded — a fix may state the old value the ledger must confirm, the from-guard.

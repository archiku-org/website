The headache

Somewhere in your organization runs software nobody fully understands anymore: its original developers are gone, its documentation is dead, and it is too critical to touch and too opaque to change.

Modernizing it feels out of reach. The knowledge left with its makers, and the resources for such a transformation simply aren't in-house. Often nobody can even say whether it's possible at all.

The deliverable

Archiku recovers that knowledge, cost-effectively. A seasoned architect and analyst, backed by an agentic workflow, digs through your codebase and produces an accelerated validated baseline analysis in days: the functional essence as standard, extended where needed with a modernization analysis or issue and bug detection.

The benefits

Every finding is traceable to the exact line of code, independently reviewed by multiple agents, with your experts in the loop as the final authority. Your team starts from a base that took days, not months.

Why Archiku

Agents excel at unstructured work: reading, researching, connecting the dots. The industry points them at generating output. Archiku points them the other way.

Archiku lets agents do what they do best. They read your entire codebase, trace behaviour to the source, map the business essence, and generate the input your people work from.

We call this software archaeology: digging through the layers of a system until the original intent surfaces again.

Meet the team

Archiku is not a toolkit. It is a team: four agents and one vault, each with its own craft, all in service of one thing. The truth about your software. Carter reads the code, Harper reads the people, Argus watches it all, Oracle speaks from the Vault. And the Vault remembers.

Carter

reads the code

Carter is a true excavator. He digs through your code and any existing documentation, layer by layer, oldest evidence first, until the original intent surfaces again. Hand Carter a system, a delivery or an entire estate and the result is the same: a map of what it really does, traceable to the exact line of code. Methodical, thorough and never in a hurry to judge.

Harper

reads the people

Harper is a true interviewer. Code does not remember everything, people do. Harper sits down with your people, in their language, and gathers the testimony the code cannot give: decisions taken, reasons forgotten, knowledge that only lives in heads. What Harper hears is checked against what Carter finds, and the gaps in the analysis close. The listener of the team.

Argus

watches it all

Argus is a true watchman. A hundred eyes, never all closed at once. He watches the codebase as it lives: every push, every change, every drift away from the baseline. What Argus sees is reported in language a board can act on. And it is Argus who keeps the Vault's knowledge up to date, so the issues it surfaces reach you before they become surprises.

Oracle

speaks from the Vault

Oracle is a true counsel. An analysis you cannot question is just a document. Oracle answers, straight from the Vault, in the language of the asker: technical for developers, business language for the board, regulatory language for compliance. Every answer carries the same traceability as the analysis it comes from. Consult the Oracle whenever reading code is not the job at hand.

The Vault

holds the universal truth

The Vault is a true memory. It is the steady state at the centre of the team. It holds the universal truth of what your software really is: every finding, every citation, every validated insight, in one place. It does not dig and it does not watch. It holds, it remembers, and because Argus never stops watching, it never goes stale. Code enters the perimeter, knowledge is all that comes out, and what the Vault holds outlives every engagement, every vendor and every reorganisation.
